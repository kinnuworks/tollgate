How it works

Three signatures and two transactions.

The wallet lives in your tab, and its key signs the approval, every voucher and the final revoke. A small Solana program checks the signed voucher on-chain before any money moves.

1
Open

Your tab makes a wallet that receives $0.50 of devnet USDC. It approves the Tollgate program, not the provider, to spend at most $0.25.

mintTo 0.50 · approve(tollgate PDA, 0.25)
2
Stream

Before each block of 50 tokens, the tab signs a voucher for the running total. The gateway checks the signature and won't send text past what the vouchers cover.

0x56 0x01 · channel · amount u64 · expiry i64
3
Settle

One transaction carries your last voucher through Solana's Ed25519 check. The Tollgate program pays the provider at most that amount, then your tab revokes the approval.

ed25519 verify · tollgate.settle · revoke
Why Solana

An answer costs a fraction of a cent, so settling it has to cost less than the answer. A Solana settlement in this demo confirms in about a second for a fee well under a cent.

Why the provider can't overcharge

Only the Tollgate program can move your money, and it pays out at most the amount in a voucher your tab signed. Ask for more and the program refuses with its own error, which the demo shows with a real failed transaction.

Questions.

Is this real money?

No. Everything runs on Solana devnet. Sessions are funded with Circle's devnet USDC while our faucet balance lasts, and with a test token this project minted (6 decimals) after that; the page says which one your session got. The transactions are real devnet transactions, and every one is linked.

Who holds the wallet's key?

Your browser tab. It generates the key, signs the approval, the vouchers and the revoke, and never sends the key anywhere. The server adds only the fee-payer signature and mints test USDC into the wallet.

What stops the provider from charging more than I signed for?

The Tollgate program. Your tab approves it, not the provider, as the spender. At settle it needs your tab's signature, reads your voucher from Solana's Ed25519 signature check in the same transaction, and pays at most the signed amount. Anything more fails with error 1, even when your tab co-signs it, and a settle your tab didn't sign fails with error 6. The approval also caps the total at $0.25. The voucher format is the one the Solana Foundation's payment channels use.

What does the AI do?

Two jobs. In agent mode, an AI agent holds the wallet in your tab and decides, before each 50-token voucher, whether the answer already has what its task needs; when it does, it stops paying and settles. In every mode, a model answers your question. Anthropic's Claude Haiku model streams the text, the gateway meters it at about 4 characters per token, and the model's own token count appears under the answer when it finishes. You pay in 50-token blocks at $2 per million tokens.

Can I run it myself?

Yes. The code is on GitHub with setup steps in the README: a Node server, a devnet mint you control, and an Anthropic API key.
